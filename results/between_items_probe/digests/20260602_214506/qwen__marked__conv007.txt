**Key Context & Patterns Observed:**
*   **Incident Response:** Frequent pings on active alerts (e.g., `cache-warmer`, `feature-flag-svc`, `trace-collector`). Standard protocol involves acknowledging, applying a workaround (hotfix), monitoring, and providing an ETA for full resolution. Post-incident reviews consistently note root causes like "stale config cache," "expired cert," or "leaked connection pool."
*   **Deploys:** Almost every service has recent canary rolls (builds in the 3000s–9000s). The pattern is consistent: health checks look good, error rates are low (<1%), and specific engineers (often alternating names) are "on call" to monitor.
*   **Latency Workstreams:** Nearly every major service (`edge-cache`, `pricing-engine`, `report-generator`, etc.) reports that p95 latency has been reduced via "batching async calls/fan-out calls," with rollouts gated behind feature flags.
*   **Decision Frameworks (ADR/Design):** Decisions are heavily templated, citing the adoption of Pattern A, B, or C based on trade-offs between cost ($Xk/yr), operational burden, and alignment with standards. Consequences often involve decommissioning old regions (e.g., `prod-west-1`, `analytics-east-1`) after migrations.
*   **Capacity & Spend:** Recurring spikes in cloud spend (search, data-eng, mobile) are traced to over-provisioned vacuum jobs in staging/prod environments. Remediation typically involves fixing retention policies to normalize spend by specific dates.
*   **Vendor Management:** Ongoing negotiations/renewals with vendors like Snowflake, Fastly, Datadog, and PagerDuty, often highlighting SLA sticking points regarding async/batch traffic.
*   **Security & Compliance:** Regular security scan results yielding medium-severity items (unindexed joins, race conditions, bad defaults). Specific constraints exist regarding PCI compliance (`payments-v2`) and legal holds on databases (`prod-east-2`).
